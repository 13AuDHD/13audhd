Making Things • September 30, 2026 • Medium read

I didn't stop writing... I got distracted building a llama.

I've skipped a few weeks of blog posts. Not because I ran out of things to say. If anything, I've had too much going on and writing a blog post kept getting pushed farther down the list.

That list has mostly had one name on it lately: Llama Scout.

What started as a relatively simple idea for sharing useful information about dispersed campsites has turned into something much bigger than I expected.

The basic idea still hasn't changed.

I wanted a way to answer the questions that normal campsite listings usually don't.

Can I actually get my vehicle down that road? How rough is it? Is there enough room to turn around? Is it level? Is there cell service? Is it private?

Are there airplanes flying overhead all afternoon?

Are there mice and other little bastards waiting to crawl into my engine compartment overnight?

Those are the things I care about when I'm looking for somewhere to stay, especially living full-time in Horizon. A pin on a map and a couple photos usually aren't enough.

Somehow that became an actual platform

Over the last several weeks I've been buried in forms, databases, admin tools, memberships, scout reports, badges, training, weather data, access information, photos, moderation tools, comparison pages, account systems, and about a thousand tiny details that nobody will ever notice unless one of them breaks.

There is now a whole Scout system for people who eventually help document places.

There's training.

There are different kinds of reports.

There are tools for suggesting edits and reporting problems.

Places can be compared against each other, and reports from different visits can show how a location changes over time.

There's even a backend I've started calling Basecamp, which might be one of my favorite accidental parts of the whole thing.

Somewhere along the way: a campsite website became a membership platform, Scout training system, moderation system, historical reporting tool, weather service, comparison tool, knowledge base, and merchandise project.

Most of the work isn't very exciting

A lot of the work lately hasn't been the exciting “look at this new feature” stuff either.

It's been things like fixing spacing.

Changing icons.

Finding weird logout bugs.

Making sure photos don't disappear.

Making sure complimentary memberships actually behave differently from free accounts.

Cleaning up wording.

Figuring out what information belongs on a campsite report and what doesn't.

Realizing that “aircraft noise” exists but I somehow forgot trains exist.

Realizing that “desert” is way too vague when southwestern Colorado and the Mojave Desert can both technically get shoved into the same category.

Building a system like this has mostly been one long chain of: “Oh, that works.” followed immediately by: “...wait.”

And then another three hours disappear.

It feels real now

Llama Scout is starting to feel less like a project I'm messing around with and more like a real thing.

The knowledge base is mostly built.

The Scout system exists.

The place reports have gotten ridiculously detailed.

Memberships work.

Weather and connectivity information are part of the system.

I've been building out badges for Scouts and certifications.

There's already merchandise work started.

Even the visual language of the site is becoming consistent instead of every page looking like it was made during a different caffeine-induced personality shift.

There's still plenty left to do, but it's far enough along now that I can actually see what it is.

That's a weird point in a project

At the beginning, everything exists in your head and it feels enormous.

Then you build pieces of it and everything looks broken.

Then eventually there's this moment where you click around and realize:

Oh.

This actually exists now.

I made this.

That's basically where I've been instead of writing blog posts.

I've also been trying to clean up some of the other digital mess in my life, deal with normal day-to-day stuff, work when I need money, and somehow keep all the other projects in my brain from wandering onto the stage while I'm trying to finish this one.

That last part is not going especially well.

But I'm trying.

Maybe weekly doesn't need to mean weekly

I don't know if I'm going to go back to posting here every single week.

I like writing these posts, but I also don't want this site to become another obligation where I feel like I failed because Tuesday came around and I didn't manufacture something worth saying.

Sometimes I'm writing.

Sometimes I'm driving around the mountains.

Sometimes I'm working.

And apparently sometimes I disappear for a few weeks and build an extremely detailed campsite intelligence platform featuring a llama.

That seems about right.

For now, though, Llama Scout is still moving forward.

And it has come a hell of a long way from the random idea it started as.
